# Playbook 5.1 — The main home, energy and water dashboards

> **For the reader.** Read this whole file before you give it to your AI
> assistant. You need Home Assistant from playbook 4.1 with some devices in
> it. An energy monitor, smart plugs with energy metering and leak sensors
> are optional: each part is skipped if you don't have the hardware. Then
> say: *"Follow this playbook. Ask me before every step marked CONFIRM."*
>
> **For the AI assistant.** You are helping a reader of *Hand-Me-Down
> Homelab* build what section 5.1 describes. Follow the rules below for the
> whole session.

## Rules for the assistant

1. **Discover, don't assume.** Use the reader's own entities, names and
   rooms. Nothing here assumes the book's devices.
2. **Show every change before you make it**, and say where it goes (which
   dashboard, which helper, which Energy setting).
3. **CONFIRM** before creating a dashboard, a helper, or changing the Energy
   configuration.
4. **Make a new dashboard rather than editing the reader's default one**,
   unless they ask otherwise.
5. **If you edit through the Home Assistant MCP server** (playbook 5.5),
   follow its write loop: read fresh, show the change, write, **read it
   back**.
6. **Verify by effect**: the page, the helper's state, the Energy
   dashboard's own validation.

## Goal: what "done" looks like

- [ ] A **Main home** dashboard in the *sections* view: one heading per
      purpose (lights, switches, climate…), tiles for things you control,
      gauges for things you watch, every card named short and explicitly.
      It reads on a phone.
- [ ] Each energy-metering plug that reports a lifetime total has a
      **daily utility meter** helper.
- [ ] The **Energy** dashboard shows whole-house use (and solar, if you
      have it) and a bar per tracked device, **with an "untracked" bar
      still there**, and its validation shows no issues.
- [ ] A **Water** dashboard whose leak section lists every leak sensor,
      found by `device_class`, not by name.

## Discover first

| What | Why | How to find it |
|---|---|---|
| Home Assistant version | The sections view needs 2024.3 or later | Settings → About, or `{{ state_attr('update.home_assistant_core_update','installed_version') }}` |
| Lights, switches and climate entities | What goes on the main-home dashboard | Settings → Entities, filtered by domain; ask which matter to the reader |
| Whole-house energy sensors | The Energy dashboard's grid (and solar) source | Entities with `device_class: energy` from the monitor's integration; ask which is "consumed from grid" and which is "solar production" |
| The monitor's own estimates | Must **not** be added as devices | Same integration, names like "always on", "other", "solar": list them for the reader |
| Plug energy sensors | Per-device bars | `{{ states.sensor | selectattr('attributes.device_class','defined') | selectattr('attributes.device_class','eq','energy') | map(attribute='entity_id') | list }}` |
| Which plug sensors are lifetime totals | Those need a daily meter | `state_class: total_increasing` and a value that never resets; Zigbee plugs' `*_summation_delivered` are |
| Leak sensors | The Water dashboard | The template in step 5 |

## Steps

### 1. The main-home dashboard — CONFIRM

Settings → Dashboards → **Add dashboard** → *New dashboard from scratch*,
named "Main home". Its first view uses the **Sections** layout. (Its URL
path must contain a hyphen, e.g. `main-home`; the interface makes one for
you, but an API or MCP call has to supply it.)

Build it by the book's rules:
- **One purpose per view.** Lights, switches and climate here; energy and
  water get their own dashboards below.
- **A heading card per section**, then the cards under it.
- **Tile cards for things you control**, gauge cards for things you watch.
- **Name every card directly** (`name:` on the card), short: a gauge clips
  its name when it's narrow, and device names are usually long.

**Verify:** the dashboard opens in the sidebar; narrow the browser window to
phone width and the sections reflow to one column with no name cut off.

### 2. A daily meter for each lifetime-total plug — CONFIRM

For each plug energy sensor that only counts up: Settings → Devices &
services → Helpers → **Create helper** → *Utility Meter*. Input sensor: the
plug's energy sensor. Meter reset cycle: **Daily**. Name it for the device
("Dryer energy today").

**Verify:** each new `sensor.*` helper exists, and its `next_reset`
attribute is the coming midnight in Home Assistant's time zone. It reads
`unknown` until the plug next reports; after that it shows kWh counted
from zero.

### 3. The Energy dashboard — CONFIRM

Settings → Dashboards → **Energy** (or Settings → Energy):
- **Electricity grid → Grid consumption:** the monitor's "consumed from grid"
  sensor. Return to grid too, if it has one.
- **Solar panels:** the monitor's solar production sensor, if you have solar.
- **Individual devices:** the plugs (or their daily meters). **Leave out the
  monitor's own estimates** ("always on", "other" and similar): they overlap
  the plugs, the device total grows past the house total, and the untracked
  bar disappears.

**Verify:** right after saving, the configuration page may warn that
statistics aren't defined yet: Home Assistant compiles them every five
minutes. After a few minutes it shows no issues. The next day, the devices
bar chart has an "Untracked" remainder, and the devices' total is below the
house total.

### 4. An always-on baseline (optional)

If the monitor provides an always-on (idle draw) figure, put it on the
Energy or Main home dashboard as a **gauge or tile** card, not as an Energy
device. It answers "what does the house draw with everything idle?".

**Verify:** the card shows a value, and step 3's device list still doesn't
include it.

### 5. Find every leak sensor by device class

In Developer tools → Template:

```
{{ states.binary_sensor | selectattr('attributes.device_class','defined')
   | selectattr('attributes.device_class','eq','moisture')
   | map(attribute='entity_id') | list }}
```

**Verify:** the count matches the leak sensors the reader physically has.
Any missing one has the wrong device class: fix it in that entity's
settings (*Show as: Moisture*), not by adding a name to a list.

### 6. The Water dashboard — CONFIRM

A new dashboard, "Water", sections view:
- **Leak sensors:** a heading and one tile per sensor from step 5.
- **Water softener, hot tub, water heater:** whatever water devices the
  reader has, each in its own section.

**Verify:** wet a leak sensor's contacts (a damp cloth) and its tile turns
to *Wet* within a few seconds, then back to *Dry*.

### 7. A full-width report page (optional)

For a page that's one long report, add a view with the **Panel** layout
holding a single vertical-stack card.

**Verify:** the stack spans the whole width.

## Gotchas (from the book and the test)

- **Device class, not names.** A leak sensor called `loftbathroom` is
  missed by any search for "water" or "leak". Every template that needs all
  leak sensors should select by `device_class: moisture`.
- **The monitor's estimates overlap the plugs.** Adding them as Energy
  devices double-counts and hides the untracked bar.
- **Zigbee plugs report a lifetime total.** Ranking devices by "today"
  needs a daily utility meter per plug.
- **Gauge names clip** when the card is narrow; name the card, not the
  device.
- **A new utility meter reads `unknown`** until its source next changes,
  and it resets at midnight in Home Assistant's time zone (Settings →
  System → General). A wrong time zone resets "today" at the wrong hour.
- **Dashboard URL paths need a hyphen.** `water` is refused;
  `dashboard-water` is fine.
- **The Energy dashboard needs statistics.** A sensor without a suitable
  `state_class` doesn't appear in its pickers; check the entity's
  attributes before assuming the integration is broken.

## Rollback

Delete the two dashboards (Settings → Dashboards), the utility meter
helpers (Settings → Helpers), and remove sources or devices from the Energy
configuration. Nothing here changes a device.
